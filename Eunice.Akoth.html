Eunice Akoth

The roots that keep me standing

Beginnings

I was born in the 1960s into a family that originally had nine children, though three of my siblings passed away before I was born. I grew up with my five surviving siblings — four brothers and one sister — and I was the fourth-born among them.

Hunger & Small Joys

Life was simple and often hard. We knew poverty intimately. Most days our meals were ugali and sukuma wiki, if we were lucky. Chapati, mandazi, or chicken were rare treasures reserved for Christmas or very special visitors — and even then, only if Dad had managed to work as a driver.

I still remember the excitement when Mum announced, "Today we are eating chapati!" We would all fight to get a piece. The smell of chapati frying in precious little oil filled the whole neighbourhood.

School & Home

We walked miles to school barefoot, our uniforms patched many times over. During the rainy season our feet were always caked in mud.

Our house was small — wooden walls and an iron-sheet roof that drummed beautifully when it rained. At night we all slept on the floor on thin mattresses squeezed together like sardines, sharing blankets. Yet those nights were full of happiness.

Sundays

Sundays were sacred. We woke up early, bathed with water warmed on the charcoal, and put on our one good set of clothes to walk to church.

The choir's singing, the clapping, the preaching — everything felt alive with the presence of God.

After service we'd all use public transport back home while our parents were left behind at church.

Discipline & Love

Dad was strict but fair. If you misbehaved, you knew the kiboko (stick) was coming, yet he always explained why. His favourite line was, "I am beating the foolishness out."

That discipline, mixed with so much love, has stayed with me all my life.

"Train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it." Proverbs 22:6

Loss

Sadly, my beloved parents, one of my brothers, and my only sister have now gone to be with the Lord. Their absence left a huge hole, and I really miss them every day. But I cling to the promise:

"O death, where is your sting?" 1 Corinthians 15:55

New Roots

Today, by God's abundant grace, I live in the United Kingdom with my husband and our three sons. We have also been blessed with a beautiful granddaughter who brings us so much joy.

The early years here were tough — the cold, the loneliness, the struggle to find work — but the Lord has been faithful. We have settled well and built a good life.
